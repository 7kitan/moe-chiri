The House Beneath the Stage

On the night the tenor disappeared, the audience blamed the rain. It had been falling since dusk, silvering the high windows of the old opera house and turning every carriage wheel into a small, black moon.

Inside, the chandeliers burned above a sea of umbrellas and silk. The orchestra played its opening measure. Then a note came from beneath the stage: one clear, trembling sound, too low for any violin and too near for a voice.

Clara heard it before anyone else. She was standing in the wings with a lantern in her hand, watching the curtain lift. Behind the painted walls, something moved through the dust. It paused beside the locked door, and three quiet knocks answered from the other side.

She looked toward the corridor. No one was there. On the floor lay a white glove, damp with rain, and a narrow card marked only with a time: 12:07.
